# UI/UX audit — 6 September 2026

## Executive assessment

The site has a credible visual foundation: restrained service colours, a white editorial canvas, clear typography, and an appropriately serious tone. Its main weakness is structural. Useful material exists, but the navigation, page hierarchy, and repeated templates make the experience feel larger without always making it easier to understand.

## Priority findings

### P0 — Navigation and orientation

- The mobile menu was not functional on the home page and was absent from internal-page headers.
- “Forces” and “Exams” previously sent visitors to one representative page rather than a comparison hub.
- Preparation pages need the same footer and trust navigation as the rest of the site.

Status: corrected with a shared responsive menu, new `/services` and `/exams` hubs, and consistent footers.

### P1 — Conversion clarity

- “Find my path” should remain the primary action; call, email, and WhatsApp are secondary contact choices.
- Each major guide should give a context-specific next step rather than relying only on a repeated contact band.
- Contact details should be visible but not compete with the educational purpose of each page.

### P1 — Demonstrated expertise

- Pages need a visible expert attribution linked to Cdr Sharma’s profile.
- His profile should explain what his experience enables him to help candidates understand, not only display four numbers.
- Distinguish factual notification information from March Ahead interpretation and practical guidance.

### P1 — Page density and repetition

- The home page is long and repeats similar promises. Condense overlapping sections and let the six preparation cards act as a clear gateway.
- Authority pages use a near-identical structure. Add page-specific comparison tables, decision aids, FAQs, and expert notes where useful.
- On mobile, reduce large headings and generous vertical spacing so visitors reach useful information sooner.

### P2 — Accessibility and polish

- Use descriptive alternative text for meaningful images and empty alternative text for decoration.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus on links, menu controls, and contact actions.
- Replace generic action labels with destinations: “Compare Army careers” is clearer than “Explore”.
- Add breadcrumbs to deep pages to improve orientation.

## Recommended experience hierarchy

1. Understand the available service and entry routes.
2. Compare eligibility using the current official notification.
3. Learn what each selection stage assesses.
4. Follow a structured preparation guide.
5. Contact the Academy for individual guidance.

## Next design pass

- Consolidate the home page onto the shared header.
- Add breadcrumbs and a compact “On this page” control to long guides.
- Build a richer Cdr Sharma profile with biography, experience timeline, teaching approach, and signed expert notes.
- Introduce page-specific modules rather than adding more general-purpose strips.
